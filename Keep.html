The Keep at the Mountain's Edge

Overview

The Keep at the Mountain's Edge began not as a fortress but as a waystation — a cluster of civilian houses gathered around the Chapel of the Deep Captain at the last reliable high ground before the eastern mountains become genuinely hostile. It grew out of a moment of ambition: a generation that believed a route through the peaks was possible, and built a permanent presence to support the expeditions that would find it.

The expeditions never came back. And then the mountains began sending things down in return.

What followed was not a planned fortification but an improvised one — walls thrown up around what already existed, barracks built against the old civilian lane, a gatehouse raised over a footpath. The chapel and its surrounding houses remain at the heart of the keep, unchanged, while everything military has been built around and between them. It is a place that wears its history in its layout: the sacred center, the civilian ring, and the garrison pressed hard against the outer walls.

Layout

The keep is divided into two distinct sections by an inner wall running east to west — the northern military enclosure built deliberately around the garrison's needs, and the southern civilian settlement that predates everything else. The Main Gate pierces the southern wall. The eastern wall of the entire keep is built directly against a cliff face.

North Section — The Garrison

The northern enclosure is ordered and deliberately arranged. The Commander's Residence sits centrally along the northern wall. The Infirmary occupies the northwest corner, with a small garden to its south. Just east of the Commander's Residence is the Mess Hall. The Barracks run along the eastern wall, and the Armory occupies the northeast tower — the largest watchtower in the keep — with a small yard in front of it. The Training Ground opens across the south-central and southeast of the enclosure, spread in front of the Commander's Residence and the Mess Hall and running west of the Barracks.

South Section — The Settlement

The southern section retains the character of the original civilian settlement. A garden sits in the northwest corner just inside the inner wall. Civilian housing clusters along the west-central and southwest. The Chapel of the Deep Captain stands at the center, with the Central Yard opening to its east. The Main Gate pierces the south wall at center — the Tavern sits to its west, and the Gatehouse to its east, with the General Store attached to the Gatehouse's western face. The Blacksmith occupies the northeast corner of the southern section. The Hunters' Hall sits east-central, between the Central Yard and the Blacksmith.

Points of Interest

North Section — The Garrison

The Commander's Residence

Two stories against the northern wall, centrally positioned so its ground-floor office has a clear view of the Training Ground and the inner gate. The office handles all formal garrison and civilian administration — correspondence, requisitions, dispute resolution — and is where Commander Bauyrzhan Dinmukhanov is most reliably found, performing the parts of command that involve sitting at a desk. A small walled garden to the south serves Lady Aigerim's afternoon gatherings more than it serves any military function.

The Infirmary

Six cots, a working stock of medicinal supplies, and an informal arrangement with the hunters to bring back any unusual finds from the forest or the lower trails. The infirmary has been busier since the mountain incursions began. A small garden to its south grows the more common medicinal herbs, tended by whoever has time.

The Mess Hall

Situated just east of the Commander's Residence, the Mess Hall feeds the garrison three times a day with little ceremony. By evening it becomes the garrison's primary social space — not quite a tavern, but close enough for soldiers who don't want to mix with civilians after a long rotation.

The Barracks

Marine garrison housing runs along the eastern wall of the northern section, with officers' quarters at the north end nearest the Armory. Run by Lieutenant Temirzhan Khazat with exacting attention — equipment inspected, rotations posted, maintenance logged. The soldiers are well-drilled and know it. What they are less certain of is whether that drilling will count for anything the next time something serious descends from the mountains.

The Armory — Northeast Tower

The largest watchtower in the keep doubles as the armory, with a small yard in front serving as an equipment staging area. Weapons, armor, and maintenance supplies are managed by a meticulous quartermaster who reports directly to the Lieutenant. The armory's records are impeccable — not a single item unaccounted for. This is Temirzhan's doing, and everyone in the garrison knows it. Permanent watch is kept from the tower's upper level, with flags and horns linking it to a secondary outpost further up the mountain trail.

The Training Ground

The open ground across the south-central and southeast of the northern enclosure is the garrison's primary training space — wide enough for formation drills, close enough to the Barracks and Mess Hall that soldiers can be pulled in quickly. Temirzhan runs rotations here every morning without exception. On quiet days the sound of sparring carries clearly into the southern section.

South Section — The Settlement

The Garden — North West

A small cultivated garden just inside the inner wall in the northwest corner of the southern section, maintained by the civilian residents. It predates the military expansion and the inner wall was built around it rather than over it, a concession that still quietly matters to the people who tend it.

Civilian Housing

The original settlement houses occupy the west-central and southwest of the southern section — stone and timber dwellings with narrow lanes between them that were never designed for marching columns. This is the part of the keep that feels least like a military installation, which is either comforting or unsettling depending on who you ask.

The Chapel of the Deep Captain

The oldest structure in the keep and the reason the settlement exists at all. Sea spirit iconography covers the interior walls and a brass compass rose is set into the stone floor — a gift from the first families who settled here, carried over the plains and through the forest on a cart. The chapel functions as the community's primary gathering space for rites of birth, marriage, and death, and has never stopped being a place of genuine devotion even as a military garrison was built around it. Some of the older townsfolk quietly resent the walls for what they imply about prayers answered.

The Central Yard

The open ground east of the chapel serves as the settlement's weekly market and general gathering space. The keep's primary well sits here. On market days the yard fills with garrison and civilians alike; the rest of the time it belongs to whoever needs it — which is usually children, traders moving stock, and hunters comparing notes near the well.

The Tavern

West of the Main Gate, the tavern is the keep's primary civilian drinking establishment and the place where opinions about the Commander circulate most freely — always quietly, always between people who already agree, always with an eye on the door. Garrison soldiers appear here in the evenings when the Mess Hall's company grows thin.

The Main Gate & Gatehouse

The Main Gate pierces the southern wall at center, the keep's primary point of entry from the forest road. The Gatehouse sits to the gate's east, housing the gate watch and logging arrivals and departures. The General Store is attached directly to the Gatehouse's western face — convenient for travelers who need to resupply immediately on arrival and haven't yet decided whether to stay.

The General Store

Attached to the western face of the Gatehouse, the store supplies travelers and soldiers with provisions, tools, and basic equipment on a civilian charter. A public noticeboard outside handles everything from lost items to expedition postings to messages too long to shout across the yard.

The Blacksmith

The forge occupies the northeast corner of the southern section, close enough to the inner wall that the garrison can commission work without crossing far into civilian territory. The smith serves both sides without friction — one of the few people in the keep that both Temirzhan and the civilian quarter regard without complaint.

The Hunters' Hall

The organizational center for the keep's civilian hunters sits east-central, between the Central Yard and the Blacksmith. Rough hand-drawn maps cover one wall, an active contracts board occupies another, and a skinning and prep area at the rear makes the building identifiable by smell before sight. The hunters range the forest and lower mountain trails and share intelligence with the garrison freely — they are often the first to know when something new has come down from the peaks, and the garrison has learned to listen.

Key Personnel

Commander Bauyrzhan Dinmukhanov

Affable, polished, and utterly convinced of his own competence, Bauyrzhan is a man who has never had to find out what he's truly capable of and has no intention of starting now. He performs command well: inspections, ceremonies, morale speeches, formal correspondence. The moment real danger enters the picture he finds a reason his presence is needed elsewhere — usually framed as letting the men handle what they are trained for.

He is the second son of a well-placed noble house, sent east to command a distant outpost that would keep him far from anything important. He arrived expecting a quiet administrative posting. Then the monsters started coming down from the mountains, and the quiet posting became something else entirely. He has adapted by continuing to act as though it's still the posting he was promised.

Lady Aigerim Dinmukhanov

Warm, cultured, and genuinely skilled at making the keep feel like a livable, civilized place — Aigerim hosts, smooths over disputes, and remembers everyone's names and their children's names. She has perfected the art of not noticing things she has decided not to notice. Mention the watchtower horn that sounded three times last week or the hunter who didn't come back, and she will redirect the conversation with practiced grace.

She is not unaware. She has simply decided that her role is to maintain the appearance of normalcy, and she performs that role with total commitment. Some townsfolk find her presence genuinely comforting — a reason to keep living ordinary lives instead of living afraid. Others find it grating, a wealthy woman's denial dressed up as civic grace.

Lieutenant Temirzhan Khazat

Sharp, relentless, and quietly exhausted, Temirzhan is the reason supplies arrive, patrols rotate, the armory is stocked, and the garrison hasn't fallen apart. He came from nothing — no family name, no connections — and rose through sheer competence. He carries himself with a confident, almost cocky front, quick with a joke and quicker with an order, because he is terrified that if he stops projecting certainty for even a moment someone will ask him to lead a fight against the things coming out of the mountains, and he has no idea if he is capable of that.

He has never been tested in real combat. He trains constantly — partly out of genuine discipline, partly because it is easier than sitting with the fear. Sooner or later something will come down from those peaks that the Commander cannot excuse himself from, and Temirzhan will have to find out, in front of everyone, whether he is actually the man he has been pretending to be.
