Glendor's Wreath

Population: ~920 (55% Human, 30% Half-Elf, 15% Gnome) Primary Trade: Lumber, charcoal burning, and woodworking. Tone: A hardy, defensive settlement at the edge of the Duskwind Forest and the Veythar Spine. Its craftspeople blend beauty with resilience, carving fine furniture and goods from timber felled in the deep wood. The village endures the storms that roll down from the mountains and the darkness that stirs at the forest's edge with equal stubbornness.

Surrounding Geography

  • The Veythar Spine: The mountain range looms to the north and east. Its lower passes are used by traders and hunters but grow dangerous in winter. Storms descend from the Spine without warning, and the villagers read the clouds as carefully as any sailor reads the sea.
  • The Duskwind Forest: Ancient and vast, the forest presses against the village's eastern palisade. The trees here grow massive, their canopy dense enough to dim midday light. It is a place of profound spiritual presence — and increasingly, of unsettling stirrings in the deeper dark.
  • Lumber Encampments: Several semi-permanent camps operate two to four miles into the forest along rutted tracks. Workers rotate between the camps and the village on a weekly schedule. The deepest camp, Deepcut, operates year-round.
  • The Lowroad: The road connecting the Lowroad villages runs along the southern foothills here. Glendor's Wreath sits at the road's midsection and functions as a waypoint and refuge for travelers moving between settlements.
  • Homesteads: Scattered farmsteads and woodcutter's holdings dot the forest edge and the open ground south of the palisade. Most are within a mile of the walls. In troubled times their inhabitants shelter inside.

Village Layout & Landmarks

  • The Ashgate – The main entrance through the palisade wall. The gate posts are permanently blackened with charcoal and oil from decades of the spring anointing ritual. Wolf skulls — real ones, honored rather than trophied — line the top of the arch. Travelers passing through for the first time are expected to pause and acknowledge the gate. Those who do not are noticed.
  • The Lumber Palisade – A stout defensive wall of felled timber encircling the village proper. Maintained constantly; the wall is a source of communal pride and practical necessity. Gaps and rot are repaired within the week they are found.
  • The Wolf Shrine
    • The central animist shrine to the Grey Wolf stands near the Ashgate — a wide flat stone beneath a break in the forest canopy, surrounded by carved wooden totems on poles. The stone is perpetually ash-stained from generations of offerings.
    • A shrine-tender lives nearby in a small attached dwelling and maintains the offerings through all seasons.
    • On still nights, villagers claim you can hear the Wolf pacing the treeline.
    • The Grey Wolf
      • Domain: War
      • Aspect: Loyalty, coordinated defense, the strength of the pack over the individual.
      • Form: A massive grey wolf with ember-lit eyes, said to walk the perimeter of the village in the hours before dawn.
      • Worship & Offerings: Soldiers, hunters, and craftspeople anoint their tools, weapons, and hands with ash and oil each spring, whispering oaths of mutual protection. Offerings of rendered fat, carved bone, and the first kill of a hunt are left at the shrine.
      • Clerical Flavor: Warriors and defenders invoke the Wolf before patrols and in moments of crisis. The phrase run with the pack is used both as a formal oath and a casual expression of solidarity.
      • Formal / Ritual Name:
        • The Grey Warden
        • He-Who-Ran-the-Pass
        • First of the Pack
      • Common / Folk Name:
        • The Wolf (most often, plainly)
        • Old Ashcoat
        • The Grey
      • Role in Worship: The Wolf is the village's protector and its standard. Legend holds that he once led the people in defending the passes against warlord marauders — running ahead of the fighters to scatter enemy scouts and howling to signal ambush positions. He is not gentle. He does not comfort. He demands readiness, loyalty, and that no one be left behind.
  • Spirit Shrines – Wolf sigil markers carved in wood and stone stand at the village gates, along forest tracks, and at the entrances to lumber encampments. Travelers leave tokens — a carved splinter, a smear of ash, a strip of cloth — when passing through.
  • The Watchpost Network – A series of elevated platforms built into the tallest trees along the palisade perimeter and extending into the surrounding forest. Staffed by rotating members of the Wreath Watch. They communicate via a system of horn signals — each call carrying a specific meaning — that villagers learn from childhood. Outsiders find the calls unsettling without context.

Government & Leadership

  • The Seat of Glendor – Leadership passes from father to child by tradition, a remnant of the Time of Warlords. The current holder does not claim kingship and does not use the title. He is called simply the Seat by those who are formal about it and by his name by everyone else. The position carries real authority — final say on defense, trade agreements, and justice — but the Seat is expected to hold open audiences at the Wreath Hall and is checked by the weight of public opinion in a community small enough that reputation is everything.
  • The Wreath Council – An informal body of senior craftspeople, the master sawyer of the Lumber Exchange, and the shrine-tender. They advise but do not vote. The Seat listens more than he is required to.
Notable Leaders:
  • Edran Gloss — The Seat (Human, Fighter 5, Battle Master) – A broad-shouldered man in his mid-forties with a calm that reads as either wisdom or suppressed temper depending on the situation. His father held the Seat before him and his father's father before that. He is not comfortable with ceremony but is very comfortable with difficult decisions. He distrusts outsiders by default and extends trust slowly and deliberately.
  • Mireth Sorn — Shrine-Tender (Half-Elf, Druid 4, Circle of the Land) – Keeper of the Wolf Shrine. Quiet, observant, and the closest thing the village has to a spiritual leader. She has begun performing cleansing rites on the forest paths more frequently than usual and has not yet told the Seat why.

Economy & Trade

  • Lumber – The primary export. Timber felled in the Duskwind Forest is processed at the Lumber Exchange and sold to traders from the Lowroad and beyond. Glendor's Wreath timber has a reputation for quality of grain and density unusual in the region.
  • Charcoal – Produced in the Charcoal Yard from lumber waste. In heavy demand from smiths in Willowford, Vey Irinel, and Freeport. The charcoal burners operate on a seasonal cycle tied to the lumber camps.
  • Carved Goods – Furniture, decorative woodwork, and carved goods represent a significant secondary trade. The village's craftspeople are known along the entire Lowroad for quality. Fine pieces occasionally reach Freeport markets.
  • Arms & Enchanted Goods – Morrow Arms has elevated the village's profile as a source of quality weapons and enchanted items, drawing buyers who would not otherwise stop in a lumber settlement.

Shops & Businesses

  • The Wreath Hall: The seat of governance and the village's communal heart. A long timber hall built from the oldest felled trees, its beams carved with generations of wolf sigils and the names of the honored dead. The Seat holds open audiences here and the village gathers for seasonal feasts and councils.
  • The Haft & Grain (General Woodworking & Trade): The village's primary woodworking shop and trading post. Fine furniture, carved household goods, tool handles, decorative pieces, and timber goods move through here. A small showroom displays finished work alongside raw materials available for purchase. Run by Oswen Dray (Gnome), a meticulous craftsman with an opinion about everything and a willingness to share it.
  • Morrow Arms: A weapons shop run by Aldren Morrow (Half-Elf, Fighter 4, Battle Master) and his wife Bolormaa Morrow (Drow, Wizard 5, School of Enchantment). Aldren is a master craftsman whose blades and hafted weapons are sought across the Lowroad. Bolormaa enchants select pieces — quietly, at her own discretion — and the shop does not advertise this service openly. Those who know, know to ask. Their working relationship is efficient and warm. Their personal relationship is visibly fond in a way neither of them calls attention to.
  • The Broken Antler Inn: The village's primary inn and tavern. Named for a hunting accident three owners ago whose details shift depending on who is telling the story. Warm, loud on market days, and reliably stocked. The current proprietor, Hess Aldric (Human), brews a dark ale called Packrun that is considerably stronger than it presents itself.
  • The Healer's Hollow: A gnome-run apothecary and healing practice built partially into the base of a massive old stump at the village's north end. Run by Pilli Voss (Forest Gnome, Druid 3, Circle of Spores), who draws on both herbalism and animist practice. The hollow beneath the stump serves as a small infirmary. Has an uneasy but functional relationship with the Seat — Pilli tends to know things Edran would prefer she didn't.
  • The Lumber Exchange: A covered open-air market structure where lumber camps bring their yields to be graded, priced, and sold. Operates on a ledger system managed by a council of three senior sawyers. Disputes over grading are common. Disputes occasionally become physical. The Exchange opens at dawn on market days and closes when the arguing stops.
  • The Charcoal Yard: A wide compound near the eastern palisade where lumber waste is burned down in covered mounds over several days. The air near the yard carries a permanent grey haze. The charcoal burners are a tight-knit group with their own internal culture and slang, considered slightly apart from the rest of the village and content to be so.
  • Deepcut Camp: The largest and most permanent of the lumber encampments, roughly two miles into the Duskwind Forest along a rutted track. More an outpost than a camp, with a bunkhouse, a tool depot, and a fire circle. The workers here are the first to notice when something is wrong in the deeper forest. They maintain their own informal hierarchy and report directly to the Seat when it matters, bypassing the Exchange entirely.

Religion & Animism

  • The people of Glendor's Wreath practice animism with a martial character shaped by the Grey Wolf. Reverence here is less gentle than in river villages — it is an exchange of oaths, a mutual obligation between the living and the spirits of the land.
  • The Wolf Shrine at the Ashgate is the primary point of communal worship. Secondary shrines stand at the lumber encampments, the Charcoal Yard, and at key points along the forest tracks where the tree canopy closes overhead and the light changes.
  • The Anointing: Each spring, villagers anoint their tools, weapons, and hands with ash and oil at the Wolf Shrine, whispering the oath to run with the pack. This is both religious rite and community bond renewal — missing the anointing is noted.
  • Shrines at Thresholds: Wolf-sigil markers stand at every significant transition point. Entering the forest without acknowledging the nearest marker is considered a provocation of bad fortune and poor manners in equal measure.
  • The Deep Forest: The village holds quiet, unspoken reverence for whatever spirits live in the oldest parts of the Duskwind Forest beyond the lumber camps. They do not name these spirits. They do not claim to know them. They leave them alone and expect to be left alone in return. This understanding has recently felt less certain than it once did.

Notable Residents

  • Edran Gloss — The Seat (Human, Fighter 5, Battle Master): Village leader by inherited position. Measured, deliberate, and protective of the village's independence. Not unkind, but not warm until earned.
  • Mireth Sorn (Half-Elf, Druid 4, Circle of the Land): Shrine-tender to the Wolf. Has been conducting quiet cleansing rites in the forest for the past several weeks and deflects questions about why.
  • Aldren Morrow (Half-Elf, Fighter 4, Battle Master): Co-owner of Morrow Arms. Precise, methodical, and proud of his craft in the way of someone who earned the pride. Has lived in Glendor's Wreath for eleven years and considers it home with a certainty that surprises people who know where he came from.
  • Bolormaa Morrow (Drow, Wizard 5, School of Enchantment): Co-owner of Morrow Arms. Watchful, dry-humored, and possessed of an unsettling ability to assess people quickly and accurately. Enchants weapons on her own terms and timeline. The village took years to warm to her. She found the process mildly interesting.
  • Pilli Voss (Forest Gnome, Druid 3, Circle of Spores): Runs the Healer's Hollow. Cheerful in a way that occasionally masks sharp concern. Has been treating an unusual number of lumber camp workers for disturbed sleep and anxiety in recent months.
  • Oswen Dray (Rock Gnome, Commoner): Master carver and proprietor of the Haft & Grain. Opinionated, exacting, and will talk at length about wood grain to anyone who makes the mistake of asking a polite question.
  • Hess Aldric (Human, Commoner): Innkeeper of the Broken Antler. Knows everything that happens in the village approximately six hours before the Seat does. Shares selectively.
  • Davan Rook (Human, Ranger 3): Senior member of the Wreath Watch and de facto commander of the watchpost network. Blunt, efficient, and deeply uncomfortable with anything he cannot see clearly. Has been filing more detailed reports than usual lately.

Festivals of Glendor's Wreath

  • Spring Equinox – The Anointing

    Renewal of oaths, sharpening of tools, and the pledge to run with the pack for another year.

    • At dawn, the village gathers at the Wolf Shrine. The shrine-tender leads the anointing — ash and oil applied to hands, tools, and weapon hilts in turn.
    • Each person speaks the oath privately, in a whisper, before the stone.
    • The Seat speaks last and aloud — the only public oath of the ceremony.
    • The afternoon is given to feasting, games of strength, and the year's first full gathering of homesteaders within the palisade.
    • Mood: Serious in the morning. Loud and celebratory by midday.
    Festival Games:
    • The Pack Run
      • Theme: Speed and endurance in honor of the Wolf.
      • Rules: A timed circuit around the outside of the palisade wall. Contestants run in pairs — one sets the pace, the other must keep it. Finishing alone disqualifies both.
      • Flavor: The point is not to win fastest. The point is to finish together. The village watches carefully for who abandons a struggling partner.
    • The Felling Contest
      • Theme: Lumber craft and raw strength.
      • Rules: Contestants are given identical pre-scored logs and compete to split them fastest. Three Athletics checks (DC 13). Best of three rounds advances.
      • Prizes: A hand-carved wolf token. The winner traditionally buys the first round at the Broken Antler.
    • The Watchpost Climb
      • Theme: Agility, nerve, and the skills of the Wreath Watch.
      • Rules: Contestants scale the tallest watchpost tree without using the built ladder rungs. Athletics or Acrobatics checks (DC 15). Highest point reached wins.
      • Flavor: Children compete on a smaller tree nearby. The Wreath Watch members traditionally do not enter but stand below and offer unhelpful commentary.
    • The Carver's Hour
      • Theme: Craft as devotion.
      • Rules: One hour to produce a wolf carving from a provided block of wood. Judged by Oswen Dray, who is exacting and makes no apologies for it. DC 14 Dexterity (Artisan's Tools) for base quality; higher results produce finer pieces.
      • Prizes: The best piece is placed at the Wolf Shrine for the year. The carver's name is added to the shrine-tender's record.
    Festival Foods & Drinks:
    • Ashbread: Dense flatbread baked with a dusting of charcoal ash on the crust, eaten with rendered fat and salt. Tastes better than it sounds.
    • Packrun Ale: Hess Aldric's dark ale, served freely at the afternoon feast. Traditionally consumed in pairs — you pour for your neighbor before yourself.
    • Smoked Venison: A haunch of deer smoked over charcoal, prepared communally and shared from a common table. Hunting the deer is an informal pre-festival tradition among the Watch.

  • Summer Solstice – The Long Watch

    Vigilance, community, and the acknowledgment that the forest is always present.

    • The Wreath Watch stands a full rotation without relief — every member at their post from dusk to dawn.
    • Villagers keep lanterns lit through the night in solidarity. No one is expected to sleep.
    • At dawn the Watch descends to a communal breakfast prepared by the rest of the village. This meal is considered one of the most important of the year.
    • Mood: Quiet and watchful through the night. Warmly exhausted and proud at sunrise.

  • Autumn Equinox – The Recall

    Return, inventory, and preparation for the months ahead.

    • Lumber camp workers return to the village for a full week. The camps are closed and the forest left alone.
    • Families take stock — of stores, of tools, of debts, of relationships. Disputes are traditionally settled at the Recall rather than carried into winter.
    • The Seat holds open audience for three consecutive days, resolving the year's outstanding grievances.
    • Mood: Businesslike, communal, and relieved. The ale flows but the conversations are serious.

  • Winter Solstice – The Grey Night

    Endurance, memory, and acknowledgment of what the dark holds.

    • No lights are lit from dusk until the Seat strikes the first fire at the Wolf Shrine at midnight. The village sits in deliberate darkness.
    • Stories are told — of the warlord raids, of the Wolf leading the defense of the passes, of those who ran with the pack and did not come home.
    • At midnight the fire is struck and every hearth in the village is lit from a shared coal carried person to person through the streets.
    • Mood: Solemn, then suddenly warm. The shift from dark to light at midnight is the emotional heart of the village year.
