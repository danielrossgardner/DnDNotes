Vae & The Transcendent Court

A closed theocratic empire to the west of Veythar, ruled by an immortal tiefling empress worshipped as the living will of the sun.

The Empress & The Jade Tower

Vae is a theocracy with the empress as the absolute center of worship. The capital rises from a plateau above a verdant valley, its skyline dominated by a jade tower at the plateau's heart — the empress's seat and the empire's sacred axis. She is known only as The Solara; her birth name is forbidden to commoners. She is believed to be immortal — whether through genuine divinity, arcane prolonging, or careful succession theater where each empress simply becomes the next is something outsiders cannot confirm. Her image is everywhere: a tiefling woman wreathed in solar imagery, horns gilded, eyes depicted as literal flames. Worship is mandatory, civic, and woven into every transaction, meal, and sunrise ritual.

The Caste Tiers

Society is rigidly stratified by blood and proximity to the tower. Tieflings occupy the upper castes, further divided by the color of their heritage — those with gold or red tones ranking above those with cooler hues. Half-breeds occupy uncomfortable middle tiers, useful but never fully trusted. At the bottom are the Shadowbound — the Tabaxi — who perform all labor the upper castes consider beneath dignity. They are not formally enslaved in the language of imperial law, which is careful about this — but their movement, marriage, and profession are all state-regulated. Rebellion is rare and punished as heresy rather than crime, which carries far worse consequences.

  • Caste 1 — The Radiant
    • The ruling class. Tieflings of gold and red heritage, closest in blood and proximity to the Solara. They occupy the highest floors of the plateau cities, hold all imperial appointments, and alone may address the Solara directly — and even then, only through prescribed channels. Their sun-discs are jade-faced with gold filigree, some set with amber or topaz. A Radiant's disc is an heirloom, not a purchase.
  • Caste 2 — The Illumined
    • The priestly and administrative class. They interpret doctrine, manage the empire's records and law, and oversee the enforcement of the Three Prayers. The Illumined are the visible face of the Solara's will in daily life — they conduct public rites, adjudicate heresy, and control what flows in and out of Sun Haven. Their sun-discs are polished brass with engraved solar rays, worn on a chain rather than carried. To remove the chain in public is a significant breach of decorum.
  • Caste 3 — The Brighthand
    • Merchants, skilled craftspeople, and sanctioned professionals. The Brighthand are the empire's economic engine, permitted moderate autonomy in trade and craft within imperial guidelines. Their sun-discs are copper or low-grade brass, plain-faced, bought rather than inherited. A Brighthand who displays a disc above their station invites scrutiny from the Illumined. Wealth is permitted. Display of wealth above caste is not.
  • Caste 4 — The Dimmed
    • Common laborers and domestic servants. The Dimmed perform the physical work of the empire's cities under the direction of higher castes. Their prayer observance is monitored more closely than any other caste, as the Illumined consider them the most spiritually vulnerable to disorder. Their sun-discs — if they have them — are stamped tin, issued rather than chosen. Many carry no disc at all, pressing bare palms together at the bells and hoping it is sufficient.
  • Caste 5 — The Shadowbound
    • The Tabaxi. State-designated as the lowest order, their caste status is inherited and immutable under imperial law. They are not permitted sun-discs. During the Three Prayers they are required to perform the posture — palms together, face toward the sun — but they are forbidden the invocation words, which are considered above their spiritual station. They mouth compliance. Beneath it, passed in whisper across generations, they keep older words alive — nature rhythms, animist fragments, teachings about magic that has nothing to do with the Solara. The Illumined know some of this persists and have never fully rooted it out, which troubles them more than they publicly acknowledge.

The Three Prayers — The Turning

The prayers of Vae are called collectively The Turning — sunrise, noon, and sunset marking the sun's arc as a living divine act performed each day by the Solara herself. To witness the sun move is to witness her will in motion. Missing a single prayer without sanctioned cause is a minor infraction logged by the nearest Illumined administrator. Missing three in succession is referred to the caste authorities.

At each prayer, bells ring across every city, town, and settlement in Vae simultaneously — a feat of coordinated signal towers that is itself presented as evidence of divine order. Citizens stop where they stand, turn to face the sun, and press both palms together raised to the forehead. The posture is called the Offered Light.

Most carry a sun-disc — a small polished token held between the pressed palms so that it catches the light during the prayer. The disc's material and ornamentation reflect caste station precisely. A Radiant's disc of jade and gold filigree and a Dimmed's stamped tin token perform the same gesture but communicate entirely different things to everyone watching. The prayers are public, communal, and observed. Performing them indoors or alone is technically permitted during illness but quietly noted.

  • The Dawn Prayer — Eternal Birth: Spoken at sunrise. The longest of the three, it recounts the Solara's rising and the defeat of the previous night's darkness. The Illumined lead a call-and-response in public squares. The Radiant conduct private dawn rites in dedicated solar chambers built into the upper floors of their estates, where the first light falls through east-facing apertures onto jade altar-discs.
  • The Noon Prayer — The Height: The briefest prayer, spoken at the sun's peak. A single invocation of six lines, recited in unison. It acknowledges the Solara's full power and asks for continued clarity — of mind, of purpose, of caste. Workers in the Dimmed districts touch fist to chest and tilt their faces skyward for a count of twelve when full recitation is impractical.
  • The Dusk Prayer — The Lasting Light: Spoken at sunset. Contemplative in tone, it thanks the Solara for the day's light and frames her descent not as an ending but as a promise — the light does not die, it waits. It is the only prayer where silence is incorporated — a held pause of several seconds before the final line. The silence is considered the most sacred moment of the day. Speaking during it is a punishable offense.

The Closed Shore

Vae does not trade openly. Foreign ships are permitted only at a single designated port on the eastern coast called Sun Haven, where all goods are inspected and all travelers confined to a walled district. No one ventures inland without an imperial escort that doubles as a warden. Exports are carefully controlled in quantity — enough to create dependency without revealing anything about the interior. What little is known of Vae has been pieced together from trade intermediaries and the rare Tabaxi who have escaped.

  • Alchemical compounds of unclear origin that apothecaries find useful but cannot reverse-engineer
  • Perfumed oils derived from rare plateau flowers unavailable elsewhere
  • Dyed silks in deep reds and blacks that hold color unlike anything Veythar weavers produce
  • Volcanic glass vessels and mirrors, prized for their clarity and dark sheen
  • Preserved spiced meats and exotic dried fruits from interior agriculture

The Sun Doctrine

The theological architecture of Vae is total. The empress is not merely favored by a god — she is the sun's will made flesh, and the sun is not a spirit in the animist sense but a sovereign force of absolute order. Darkness, disorder, and silence are heresy. The Tabaxi's closeness to nature and animist tendencies are framed in imperial doctrine as proof of their spiritual inferiority — they hear echoes because they cannot bear the full light.

Magic in Vae flows through the Solara. It is granted, not learned — awarded to the Illumined as an extension of her divine sanction and revoked if that sanction is withdrawn. No caste below the Illumined is permitted to study or practice arcane arts. That the Shadowbound Tabaxi nonetheless carry fragments of nature magic — older, quieter, tied to no empire and no empress — is the doctrine's most persistent and unresolved problem. The Illumined frame it as corruption. They have not managed to eliminate it.

Scholars in Veythar who have studied fragments of Vae texts note an unsettling tension: the doctrine is obsessively focused on suppressing something, though what specifically is never named directly. Only that the flame must never go out.
